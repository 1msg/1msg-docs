---
title: Customer consent (opt-in)
description: Why you need a customer's consent before you message them first on WhatsApp, and who is responsible for it.
---

Before you message a customer on WhatsApp first, you need their consent. This is a Meta requirement: marketing templates may only be sent to people who have **explicitly agreed** to receive messages.

Consent is not “turned on” anywhere in the product — the platform does not store or verify it. Collecting it, keeping a record, and being able to prove it is the business’s responsibility.

## Who is responsible

Responsibility for obtaining valid consent rests with the platform customer. This is stated in our terms: the user is responsible for complying with WhatsApp Business Terms, Meta policies, anti-spam laws, and marketing communication rules.

1MSG does not influence Meta’s decisions — not account approval, not sending limits, not quality rating, and not blocks.

## What you must not do

Using the service prohibits:

* sending unsolicited or unlawful messages;
* working with purchased or unauthorized contact lists;
* violating applicable anti-spam laws;
* circumventing opt-in requirements;
* engaging in misleading communications.

## What happens without consent

Messages to people who did not expect them lead to spam complaints. Consequences come from Meta’s side:

* the number’s quality rating drops;
* sending limits are reduced;
* the number may be restricted or blocked.

Explicit consent lowers the risk of complaints and helps avoid a number block.

## How this relates to the 24-hour window

Until the customer messages you first, the window is closed — the first message is sent as a template. When the customer replies, the window opens, and you can continue with free-form text.
